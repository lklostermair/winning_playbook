# Remedies, Liability & Warranty

## Standard Position
Confidential Information is provided 'AS IS' with no warranty as to its accuracy, completeness, or fitness for any purpose. A contractual penalty may be specified for a material breach of the agreement.

## Fallback Position
- **Warranty:** Include a good faith representation that CI is not knowingly misleading (A5) or explicitly state no liability for damages arising from reliance on CI (A4).
- **Contractual Penalty:** Negotiate whether the penalty is the 'sole and exclusive monetary remedy' (A4) or 'shall not constitute the sole remedy' (A5), allowing for additional actual damages. Negotiate the specific penalty amount and aggregate cap.
- **Liability:** Negotiate explicit exclusion of indirect, incidental, special, punitive, exemplary, or consequential damages. Negotiate a maximum aggregate liability cap for direct damages.

## Red Line
Unlimited liability for all types of damages. A contractual penalty that is so low it's not a deterrent. A warranty of accuracy or completeness for Confidential Information.

## Decision Logic
The 'sole remedy' vs. 'non-sole remedy' for contractual penalties is a critical negotiation point. As a Disclosing Party, prefer the penalty to be non-exclusive to preserve the right to claim actual damages. As a Receiving Party, prefer it to be the sole monetary remedy. Always aim to limit indirect/consequential damages and cap direct damages.

## Escalation Logic
Escalate if the counterparty insists on unlimited liability, demands a warranty of CI accuracy, or proposes a penalty clause that is clearly unfavorable without other liability limitations.

## Rationale
This area shows the most significant differences between the contracts. A5 allows for indirect/consequential damages and actual damages beyond the penalty. A4 limits liability to direct damages with a cap and makes the penalty the sole monetary remedy. A4 also explicitly excludes liability for reliance on CI, while A5 includes a good faith representation.

## Negotiation Tips
- Prioritize excluding indirect, incidental, special, punitive, exemplary, and consequential damages.
- Aim for a reasonable cap on direct damages.
- If a contractual penalty is included, clarify whether it is the sole monetary remedy or if other damages can also be claimed. This is a major point of financial exposure.

## Suggested Language
(Warranty) 'All Confidential Information is provided 'AS IS.' The Disclosing Party makes no warranty as to its accuracy, completeness, or fitness for any purpose. [However, each Party represents in good faith that, to the best of its knowledge as of the date of each disclosure, the Confidential Information provided is not knowingly misleading in any material respect. / Neither Party shall be liable for any damages arising from the other Party's use of or reliance on the Confidential Information.]' (Penalty/Liability) 'In the event of a material breach of this Agreement that is proven by the non-breaching Party, the breaching Party shall pay to the non-breaching Party a contractual penalty of [AMOUNT] per proven breach, up to a maximum aggregate amount of [MAX AMOUNT]. [The contractual penalty shall not constitute the sole remedy and shall be without prejudice to the non-breaching Party's right to claim additional actual damages and to seek injunctive or equitable relief. / Payment of the contractual penalty shall constitute the sole and exclusive monetary remedy for such breach, without prejudice to the non-breaching Party's right to seek injunctive or equitable relief.] Each Party's liability for any breach of this Agreement shall be limited to direct damages actually incurred by the non-breaching Party, [up to a maximum aggregate amount of [AMOUNT]]. Neither Party shall be liable for punitive, exemplary, speculative, indirect, incidental, special, or consequential damages.'
