# Choice of Law (Governing Law)

## Standard Position
Governing law of our own jurisdiction

## Fallback Position
- Governing law of a neutral third jurisdiction that is well-established, commercially sophisticated, and similar to our own (e.g., Swiss law for European parties, English law for international agreements)
- Accept the counterparty's governing law if it is a well-established commercial jurisdiction and the NDA is otherwise balanced

## Red Line
Reject governing law of jurisdictions with unpredictable legal systems, weak IP protection, or where enforcement of confidentiality obligations is uncertain

## Decision Logic
The governing law determines how the NDA will be interpreted and enforced. It also often sets a precedent for the governing law of any subsequent commercial agreement. Each party naturally prefers its own jurisdiction's laws. Counterparties will typically impose their own law. In cross-border NDAs, this can create disadvantages if the chosen law is unfamiliar, unpredictable, or materially different from our own jurisdiction's approach to confidentiality and IP protection.

## Escalation Logic
If the proposed governing law is from a jurisdiction outside of our organization's country of incorporation or primary place of business, escalate for assessment.
