# Other Liabilities (Indemnification, Limitation of Liability)

## Standard Position
No specific indemnification clause in the NDA. Liability governed by applicable law (which generally provides for unlimited liability for breach)

## Fallback Position
- Accept a reasonable limitation of liability (e.g., exemption for slight negligence) that applies equally to both parties
- Accept an indemnification clause limited to direct damages only, with a reasonable cap, and excluding consequential, indirect, special, and punitive damages

## Red Line
Reject any one-sided indemnification, any inclusion of punitive or consequential damages, or any indemnity that extends to third-party claims unrelated to the NDA

## Decision Logic
The liability framework in an NDA determines the financial exposure in case of breach. While some level of liability is appropriate to give the NDA teeth, overly broad indemnification clauses or the inclusion of consequential/punitive damages can create disproportionate risk. Some NDAs include broad indemnification clauses requiring one party to hold the other harmless against "any and all claims, damages, losses, costs, and expenses" — including indirect, consequential, special, and punitive damages. Others may attempt to limit the disclosing party's liability while leaving the receiving party's liability unlimited.

## Escalation Logic
Any indemnification clause or liability provision that goes beyond standard statutory liability should be escalated.
