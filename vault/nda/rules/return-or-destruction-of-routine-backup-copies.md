# Return or Destruction of Routine Backup Copies

## Standard Position
Return or destruction upon request, with explicit exemptions for: (a) routine IT backup copies, and (b) copies required to be retained under applicable law — both of which remain subject to ongoing confidentiality obligations

## Fallback Position
- Return or destruction upon request, with an exemption for legally required copies only (no explicit IT backup exemption, but a general "commercially reasonable efforts" standard for destruction)
- Return or destruction upon request with certification, but with a reasonable timeframe (e.g., 60 days) to comply

## Red Line
Reject any clause requiring immediate destruction of all copies with no exemptions — this is technically and legally impossible to guarantee

## Decision Logic
Organizations routinely create backup copies of data as part of standard IT operations, and certain records must be retained under applicable laws and regulations. An NDA that requires the return or destruction of all copies — without exception — creates an impossible compliance situation. Some NDAs require the return or destruction of "all copies, in any form" without exempting routine IT backups or legally mandated archives. Compliance with such a clause may be technically impossible or illegal.

## Escalation Logic
If the counterparty insists on destruction of all copies including backups with written certification, escalate.
