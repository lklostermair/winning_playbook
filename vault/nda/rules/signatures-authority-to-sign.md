# Signatures and Authority to Sign

## Standard Position
Both parties sign through duly authorized representatives with verified signing authority. For our organization, follow internal policies on required number of signatories and authority levels

## Fallback Position
- Accept electronic signatures via established e-signature platforms, provided the counterparty confirms the signatory's authority in writing
- Accept a single signatory from the counterparty if they provide evidence of signing authority (e.g., power of attorney, board resolution)

## Red Line
Do not execute any NDA where the counterparty's signatory authority cannot be verified

## Decision Logic
_Not specified._

## Escalation Logic
If there is any doubt about the counterparty's signatory authority, escalate before execution.

## Rationale
An NDA is only binding if signed by individuals who are legally authorized to represent their respective organizations. If a signatory lacks authority, the NDA may be invalid, leaving disclosed information unprotected.

## Negotiation Tips
- Counterparties sometimes present NDAs signed by individuals whose authority is unclear (e.g., a project manager rather than a corporate officer). Additionally, our own internal signing authority rules must be followed.
